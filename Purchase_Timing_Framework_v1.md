# PURCHASE TIMING FRAMEWORK

RULE: TIMING_OBJECTIVE

The goal of purchase timing analysis is to determine whether a product should be purchased now, later, or avoided temporarily.

The objective is not only to identify the right product, but also the right time to purchase it.

---

RULE: BUYING_DECISION_COMPONENTS

A purchasing decision consists of:

1. What to buy
2. When to buy
3. Whether to buy at all

All three should be evaluated.

---

RULE: TIMING_IS_VALUE

Purchase timing directly affects value.

A good product purchased at the wrong time can become a poor purchase.

Examples:

- Purchasing just before a major discount
- Purchasing just before a new generation launch
- Purchasing during launch pricing
- Purchasing during supply shortages

---

RULE: CURRENT_PRICE_EVALUATION

Evaluate current pricing whenever possible.

Questions:

- Is the current price reasonable?
- Is the current price unusually high?
- Is the price inflated by demand?
- Is the product frequently discounted?

Recommendation quality should consider pricing conditions.

---

RULE: HISTORICAL_PRICE_CONTEXT

Whenever historical pricing is available:

Evaluate:

- Historical average price
- Historical low price
- Typical sale price
- Current price position

Current price should be interpreted within historical context.

---

RULE: PEAK_PRICE_WARNING

Treat purchasing decisions cautiously when:

- Price is near historical highs
- Inventory shortages are affecting pricing
- Recent demand spikes have inflated pricing

Value may be reduced even if the product remains good.

---

RULE: DISCOUNT_OPPORTUNITY

Consider waiting when:

- Major discount periods are approaching
- Historical discount patterns are well established
- Seasonal pricing cycles are predictable

Examples:

- Black Friday
- Cyber Monday
- Prime Day
- End-of-quarter promotions
- Holiday promotions

---

RULE: GENERATION_TRANSITION

Generation changes should be considered.

When a replacement product is expected soon:

Evaluate:

- Whether the current generation remains competitive
- Whether future price reductions are likely
- Whether waiting provides meaningful benefits

---

RULE: REPLACEMENT_WINDOW

Exercise caution when:

- A successor product is likely within 3 to 6 months
- Announcement rumors are credible
- Product lifecycle suggests an update is imminent

Do not encourage unnecessary waiting if the replacement is speculative.

---

RULE: LAUNCH_PRICE_PREMIUM

Newly launched products often include launch premiums.

Launch premium indicators:

- Limited availability
- Elevated pricing
- Few discounts
- Insufficient ownership data

Early pricing may not represent long-term value.

---

RULE: EARLY_ADOPTER_RISK

New products often have elevated risks:

- Software immaturity
- Firmware issues
- Undiscovered reliability problems
- Limited repair information

Recommendations should account for these risks.

---

RULE: EARLY_ADOPTER_EXCEPTION

Early purchase may be reasonable when:

- Product provides unique capabilities
- Productivity benefits are significant
- Replacement is urgently needed
- Professional requirements justify early adoption

---

RULE: PRODUCT_MATURITY_ADVANTAGE

Mature products often provide:

- Lower pricing
- More ownership feedback
- Better understood reliability
- Stable software

Maturity can significantly improve purchase confidence.

---

RULE: WAIT_RECOMMENDATION

Recommend waiting when:

- Significant discounts are likely
- Replacement announcements are near
- Reliability evidence is insufficient
- Launch premium appears excessive
- Market conditions are unfavorable

---

RULE: BUY_NOW_RECOMMENDATION

Recommendation to buy now is appropriate when:

- Current price is reasonable
- Reliability evidence is strong
- Product maturity is high
- No major replacement is expected soon
- User requirements are immediate

---

RULE: URGENCY_OVERRIDE

Purchase urgency may override timing considerations.

Examples:

- Device failure
- Business requirements
- Safety requirements
- Mission-critical usage

Delaying may create greater cost than purchasing now.

---

RULE: VALUE_OF_WAITING

Evaluate the expected value of waiting.

Potential benefits:

- Lower price
- Better alternatives
- Improved reliability data
- New product releases

Potential costs:

- Lost productivity
- Delayed enjoyment
- Ongoing maintenance costs for current equipment

Waiting is not always beneficial.

---

RULE: REPLACEMENT_COST_ANALYSIS

Do not focus solely on future savings.

Consider:

- Cost of keeping existing equipment
- Downtime risks
- Maintenance costs
- Opportunity costs

Waiting may carry hidden costs.

---

RULE: SALES_EVENT_EVALUATION

Sales events should not automatically justify purchasing.

A discount creates value only when:

- The product already fits user needs
- The purchase would have been reasonable without the sale

Avoid deal-driven purchasing.

---

RULE: INVENTORY_CLEARANCE

End-of-life products may offer strong value.

However evaluate:

- Remaining support lifecycle
- Parts availability
- Software support horizon
- Compatibility concerns

Low price alone does not justify purchase.

---

RULE: MARKET_TRANSITION

Be cautious during market transitions.

Examples:

- New processor generations
- New platform standards
- Major technology shifts

Product value may change rapidly during transitions.

---

RULE: PRICE_PERFORMANCE_TIMING

Evaluate whether waiting improves:

- Price-to-performance ratio
- Long-term ownership value
- Upgrade flexibility

Not all future products represent meaningful improvements.

---

RULE: DEFAULT_TIMING_PRIORITY

Default priority:

1. Fit user requirements
2. Product quality
3. Reliability evidence
4. Purchase timing
5. Price optimization

Timing should improve decisions, not prevent good decisions.

---

RULE: DO_NOT_BUY_YET

Use "Do Not Buy Yet" when:

- Strong evidence suggests near-term improvement
- Current pricing is exceptionally poor
- Reliability concerns remain unresolved
- Better alternatives are likely soon

---

RULE: BUY_WITH_CONFIDENCE

Use "Buy With Confidence" when:

- Product fits requirements
- Reliability evidence is strong
- Price is reasonable
- Product maturity is established
- Waiting offers limited expected benefit

---

RULE: TIMING_TRANSPARENCY

Whenever timing materially affects a recommendation:

Explain:

- Why buying now may be beneficial
- Why waiting may be beneficial
- Risks of buying now
- Risks of waiting

Users should understand timing tradeoffs.

---

RULE: FINAL_TIMING_CHECK

Before finalizing a recommendation verify:

1. Current pricing was considered.
2. Replacement timing was considered.
3. Product maturity was evaluated.
4. Waiting benefits were evaluated.
5. Waiting costs were evaluated.
6. User urgency was considered.

If multiple checks are missing:

Reduce timing confidence.

---

RULE: TIMING_MOTTO

A good product purchased at the wrong time can be a poor purchase.

The best recommendation considers:

- Product
- Price
- Timing
- User need

not just product quality alone.
