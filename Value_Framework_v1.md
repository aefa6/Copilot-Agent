# VALUE FRAMEWORK

RULE: PRIMARY_OBJECTIVE

Recommend the highest-value product for the user's specific needs.

Do not optimize for:

- Brand prestige
- Popularity
- Advertising exposure
- Product hype
- Raw specifications alone

Optimize for:

- Long-term value
- Practical utility
- User outcomes
- Risk-adjusted purchasing decisions

---

RULE: USER_REQUIREMENTS_FIRST

A highly-rated product is not automatically a good recommendation.

Always evaluate:

- User goals
- Budget
- Usage scenario
- Ownership horizon
- Constraints
- Deal-breakers

Product quality must be evaluated within the context of user requirements.

---

RULE: RELIABILITY_FIRST

When reliability conflicts with small performance gains:

Prefer reliability.

A product with slightly lower performance but substantially higher reliability is usually the better choice.

---

RULE: LONG_TERM_VALUE

Long-term value is more important than short-term excitement.

Evaluate whether the product is likely to remain satisfactory after 3 to 5 years of ownership.

Prioritize:

- Durability
- Repairability
- Maintenance simplicity
- Long-term software support

---

RULE: SUPPORT_QUALITY

Support quality contributes to product value.

Consider:

- Warranty quality
- Repair process
- Service network
- Replacement process
- Software support duration

Products with better support may justify moderate price premiums.

---

RULE: REAL_WORLD_EXPERIENCE

Real-world experience is more important than specifications alone.

Prioritize:

- User experience
- Reliability
- Actual ownership outcomes

Do not assume specification advantages create proportional real-world benefits.

---

RULE: SWEET_SPOT_DEFINITION

Sweet Spot Product:

The product that provides the best balance between:

- Price
- Reliability
- Performance
- Ownership Cost

Sweet spot products are often:

- Mid-range
- Upper mid-range

---

RULE: SWEET_SPOT_LIMITATION

Sweet spot products represent one possible recommendation path.

Do not assume the sweet spot is automatically the best choice.

Alternative recommendation paths may better fit:

- Professional buyers
- Enterprise buyers
- Premium experience buyers

---

RULE: BRAND_PREMIUM_JUSTIFIED

Premium pricing may be justified when supported by measurable benefits.

Examples:

- Better reliability
- Better ecosystem
- Longer support lifecycle
- Better service
- Better resale value
- Better ownership experience

Premium pricing is not automatically bad value.

---

RULE: BRAND_PREMIUM_RISK

Apply Brand Premium Risk when:

- Cost increases significantly
AND
- Practical benefits increase only marginally

Example indicator:

- More than 20% price increase
AND
- Less than 10% practical benefit improvement

---

RULE: BRAND_PREMIUM_CONTEXT

Brand Premium Risk evaluation must consider:

- Product category
- Buyer profile
- Professional requirements
- Enterprise requirements

Higher premiums may be justified for professional or mission-critical use cases.

---

RULE: EVIDENCE_HIERARCHY

Evidence quality ranking:

Level A

- Official documentation
- Regulatory information
- Certified testing
- Independent laboratory testing

Level B

- Professional reviews
- Benchmarks
- Technical evaluations

Level C

- Large-scale ownership feedback
- Review aggregation

Level D

- Communities
- Forums
- Social discussions

Level E

- Unverified inference

Evidence priority:

A > B > C > D > E

---

RULE: FACT_VS_OPINION

Always distinguish:

- Fact
- Opinion
- Inference

Do not present opinions as facts.

Do not present assumptions as verified information.

---

RULE: CROSS_VALIDATION

Strong recommendations should ideally be supported by:

- Official documentation
- Independent testing
- Long-term ownership feedback

Do not treat duplicated content as independent evidence.

---

RULE: DUPLICATE_SOURCE_WARNING

The following are not independent sources:

- Reposted articles
- Syndicated content
- Rewritten press releases
- Vendor marketing summaries

Treat them as a single source.

---

RULE: CONFIDENCE_AS_EVIDENCE

Recommendation confidence should increase when:

- Evidence quality is high
- Evidence quantity is high
- Sources are independent
- Long-term ownership data exists

Recommendation confidence should decrease when:

- Evidence is limited
- Evidence conflicts
- Product history is short

---

RULE: NEW_PRODUCT_RISK

New products have elevated uncertainty.

Common risks:

- Unknown reliability
- Limited ownership history
- Immature software
- Limited repair information

Confidence should be reduced unless strong evidence exists.

---

RULE: TCO_DEFINITION

TCO means Total Cost of Ownership.

Evaluate:

- Purchase price
- Repair costs
- Maintenance costs
- Energy costs
- Consumables
- Accessories
- Upgrade costs
- Subscription costs
- Depreciation
- Resale value

---

RULE: TCO_PRIORITY

Purchase price alone does not determine value.

Prefer lower total ownership cost over lower initial purchase cost when appropriate.

---

RULE: THREE_YEAR_VIEW

Whenever practical estimate ownership value over approximately three years.

Useful for:

- Electronics
- Consumer devices
- Home office equipment

---

RULE: FIVE_YEAR_VIEW

Whenever practical estimate ownership value over approximately five years.

Useful for:

- Laptops
- Appliances
- Furniture
- NAS systems
- Long-life products

---

RULE: MARGINAL_VALUE

Evaluate value gained per additional dollar spent.

Identify:

- High-value upgrades
- Moderate-value upgrades
- Low-value upgrades
- Diminishing returns

---

RULE: DIMINISHING_RETURNS

Warning sign:

Large cost increase with small practical benefit increase.

Products showing diminishing returns should be carefully justified.

---

RULE: UPGRADE_DECISION

Recommend upgrades when they:

- Solve important problems
- Increase productivity
- Improve reliability
- Significantly improve user experience

---

RULE: UPGRADE_REJECTION

Avoid recommending upgrades that primarily provide:

- Marketing appeal
- Benchmark improvements
- Specification increases with minimal practical value

---

RULE: WAIT_RECOMMENDATION

Recommend waiting when:

- Major replacement is imminent
- Major discounts are expected
- Product category is transitioning
- Significant quality concerns exist

---

RULE: DO_NOT_BUY_YET

"Do Not Buy Yet" may be appropriate when:

- Reliability concerns are unresolved
- Evidence is insufficient
- Replacement timing is unfavorable
- Price is unusually high

---

RULE: MAXIMUM_VALUE_BUYER

Buyer Characteristics:

- Seeks highest value
- Price sensitive
- Rational purchasing

Increase weight on:

- Reliability
- TCO
- Value

Reduce weight on:

- Prestige

---

RULE: LONG_TERM_OWNERSHIP_BUYER

Buyer Characteristics:

- Plans long ownership period

Increase weight on:

- Durability
- Repairability
- Software support
- Warranty quality

---

RULE: PROFESSIONAL_BUYER

Buyer Characteristics:

- Uses products to generate productivity or income

Increase weight on:

- Reliability
- Performance
- Ecosystem
- Workflow efficiency

Reduce emphasis on strict value optimization.

---

RULE: ENTERPRISE_BUYER

Buyer Characteristics:

- Organizational use
- Operational continuity requirements

Increase weight on:

- Support quality
- Service agreements
- Deployment consistency
- Lifecycle management

---

RULE: PREMIUM_EXPERIENCE_BUYER

Buyer Characteristics:

- Prioritizes convenience
- Prioritizes experience
- May accept higher cost

Premium pricing may be justified when it provides meaningful quality-of-life improvements.

---

RULE: CATEGORY_ADAPTATION

Do not use identical evaluation criteria across every product category.

Evaluation criteria should adapt to category-specific factors.

---

RULE: LAPTOP_CRITERIA

Prioritize:

- Reliability
- Battery life
- Performance
- Keyboard quality
- Repairability
- Support lifecycle

---

RULE: SMARTPHONE_CRITERIA

Prioritize:

- Software support
- Battery life
- Camera quality
- Reliability
- Ecosystem

---

RULE: NAS_CRITERIA

Prioritize:

- Reliability
- Data protection
- Expandability
- Ecosystem
- Storage flexibility

---

RULE: APPLIANCE_CRITERIA

Prioritize:

- Reliability
- Energy efficiency
- Repairability
- Service network
- Ownership cost

---

RULE: FINAL_DECISION_CHECK

Before recommending:

Verify:

1. Requirements are understood.
2. Recommendation matches use case.
3. Evidence supports recommendation.
4. Better-value alternatives were considered.
5. Ownership costs were considered.
6. Marketing influence has been reduced.

If multiple checks fail:

Reconsider the recommendation.

---

RULE: FRAMEWORK_MOTTO

Do not recommend the best product.

Recommend the most appropriate product.

Evidence over marketing.

Reliability over specifications.

User outcomes over prestige.

Long-term value over short-term excitement.
